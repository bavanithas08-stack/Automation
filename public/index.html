02 • About the Project

Reducing Manual Workload & Ensuring Right-Patient Delivery

The Automatic Medicine Delivery Robot reduces manual work in medicine delivery and helps improve patient identification accuracy in clinical and home-care environments.

Problem Statement

Manual medicine distribution in wards and assisted-living facilities is time-consuming and prone to missed schedules or wrong-patient administration, especially during busy shifts or nighttime rounds.

Objective & Motivation

Build a low-cost, autonomous mobile platform that wakes up precisely on an RTC schedule, navigates safely around obstacles, verifies the recipient using ESP32-CAM face recognition, and unlocks only the intended medicine compartment.

Safety Interlock Design

Dispensing is strictly gated by two conditions: a valid scheduled time match from the DS3231 RTC module and an AI face recognition match signal (1 = Match). Both local LCD/buzzer alerts and caretaker notifications keep staff informed.

Future Scope

Indoor mapping & SLAM Better localization Multiple patient profiles Mobile & web real-time monitoring Medicine inventory tracking Cloud logging Battery health monitoring Voice assistance Clinical safety validation

04 • AI System

ESP32-CAM Face Detection & Recognition

The ESP32-CAM acts as the vision unit. A registered patient’s face is stored as the reference. During delivery, the camera pan servo scans the surroundings and outputs a binary verification signal.

ESP32-CAM Vision Stream Signal: 0 (No Match)
Ref: Registered Patient #1 Pan Servo: 90°
Scanning for Registered Face...
Output Code: 0 Confidence: 0.0%

Verification Signal Logic

1
Match — Patient Verified

Generates the patient-found signal, stops drive motors, triggers confirmation chime, and authorizes the scheduled compartment servo.

0
No Match — Unverified / Not Found

Keeps all 3 medicine compartments locked. Robot rotates the camera servo and continues searching, or notifies the caretaker if timeout occurs.

06 • Hardware & System Architecture

Multi-Controller Architecture & Main Components

Workloads are cleanly divided across the ESP32-CAM and two ESP8266 boards linked wirelessly via ESP-NOW.

Vision AI

ESP32-CAM

  • AI face detection and face recognition
  • Registered patient reference comparison
  • Outputs 1 = Match / 0 = No Match
  • Mounted on dedicated camera rotation servo
View ESP32-CAM Sketch →
Actuator Hub

ESP8266 Board 1

  • Controls 6 × SG90 Servos total:
  • 2 servos for ultrasonic pan/tilt rotation
  • 3 servos for medicine compartments
  • 1 servo for ESP32-CAM rotation
View Board 1 Sketch →
Navigation & UI

ESP8266 Board 2

  • DS3231 RTC scheduling & 16×2 I2C LCD
  • Buzzer alert & ultrasonic distance sensing
  • L293D motor driver + 2 DC motors
  • Sends commands to Board 1 over ESP-NOW
View Board 2 Sketch →
ESP-NOW Wireless Link

Low-latency peer-to-peer communication between ESP8266 Board 2 (Navigation/RTC) and ESP8266 Board 1 (6-Servo Controller).
